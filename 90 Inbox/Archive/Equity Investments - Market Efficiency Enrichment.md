# Equity Investments - Market Efficiency Enrichment

## Rationale

The deck gives exam-relevant implications, anomaly categories, and behavioral explanations that the current atomic definitions do not preserve.

## Exact proposed content

Enrich `Informational Market Efficiency`: new information is reflected quickly and rationally; passive investing is preferred to active investing because of lower costs; prices react only to unexpected information; and, in the slide's framework, market prices accurately reflect intrinsic values. Factors include participant count, information availability, limits to trading, transaction costs, and information-acquisition costs.[^eq-eff-p29]

Enrich the three forms:

- `Weak-Form Market Efficiency`: prices reflect historical prices and volume, so technical analysis cannot consistently produce abnormal profit.
- `Semi-Strong-Form Market Efficiency`: prices accurately and quickly reflect all public information, so fundamental analysis cannot consistently produce abnormal profit; this form includes weak form.
- `Strong-Form Market Efficiency`: prices reflect public and private information, so insiders cannot consistently earn abnormal returns from private information; this form includes semi-strong and weak forms.[^eq-eff-p30]

Enrich anomaly notes with the warning that persistent anomalies are exceptions to efficiency but some findings may be products of data mining or data snooping.[^eq-eff-p31]

Enrich `Calendar Anomaly` with January or turn-of-the-year, turn-of-the-month, day-of-the-week, weekend, and holiday effects. The slide links the January effect to tax-loss selling and window dressing; turn-of-the-month covers the last trading day and first three trading days of the next month.[^eq-eff-p31][^eq-eff-p32]

Enrich `Momentum Anomaly` and `Overreaction`: recent high-return securities tend to continue producing high returns in subsequent short periods, while overreaction can inflate prices after good information or depress them after bad information.[^eq-eff-p32]

Enrich `Size Effect`, `Value Effect`, `Closed-End Fund Discount`, and `IPO Anomaly`: small-cap stocks are presented as outperforming on a risk-adjusted basis; value stocks with below-average P/E and P/B ratios as outperforming growth stocks; closed-end funds as commonly trading below NAV; unexpected earnings as followed by slow price adjustment; and IPO allocations at the offer price as potentially producing abnormal profits.[^eq-eff-p33]

Enrich behavioral notes:

- `Overconfidence Bias`: too much confidence in information-processing ability can contribute to mispricing.
- `Representativeness Bias`: new information and outcome probabilities are assessed by similarity to a current state or familiar classification.
- Add loss aversion to the nearest existing behavioral concept or create an atomic proposal only if alias review confirms it is absent: investors dislike comparable losses more than gains.
- Market efficiency requires the market, not each participant, to be rational.[^eq-eff-p34]

Enrich `Information Cascade`, `Herding`, `Mental Accounting`, `Conservatism Bias`, and `Narrow Framing`:

- An information cascade transmits information from early actors whose decisions influence others; imitation of informed traders can be rational.
- Herding is clustered trading not necessarily driven by information.
- Mental accounting separates investments into differently treated mental accounts.
- Conservatism is slow reaction to new information and continued reliance on prior views.
- Narrow framing considers issues in isolation and reacts to how they are posed.[^eq-eff-p35]
