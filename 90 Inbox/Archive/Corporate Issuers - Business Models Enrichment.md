# Corporate Issuers - Business Models Enrichment

## Rationale

The current chapter map captures several model names but misses many explicitly named pricing and network structures. Existing notes also repeat a generic description instead of explaining what each model changes about customers, revenue, cost, or scale.

## Exact proposed content

### Value proposition and channels

Enrich `Business Model` and `Value Proposition` using the source's four questions: who are the customers, what product or service is offered, where and through which channels it reaches them, and how much/how customers pay. These choices jointly define how a company creates and captures value.[^ci-bm-p42]

Enrich `Distribution Channel` with traditional channel strategy, direct sales versus agency arrangements, and omnichannel distribution. The exam-relevant distinction is whether the issuer owns the customer transaction, relies on agents, or coordinates several channels.[^ci-bm-p42]

### Pricing architecture

Enrich `Pricing Power`: commodity producers with many competitors and little product differentiation are price takers with limited pricing power and commonly compete through cost leadership.[^ci-bm-p42]

Create the following atomic pricing concepts because each is explicitly named in the review deck and is a useful comparison target:

- `Value-Based Pricing`: price is anchored to perceived customer value.
- `Cost-Based Pricing`: price is anchored to cost plus a required margin.
- `Tiered Pricing`: a form of price discrimination using defined product or usage tiers.
- `Reverse Auction`: sellers compete to offer the lowest acceptable price, contrasting with a conventional auction.
- `Add-On Pricing`: a core offering is supplemented by separately priced additions.
- `Hidden Revenue Business Model`: users may not be the direct payer; revenue comes from another party or activity.
- `Recurring Revenue Model`: customers pay repeatedly over time; `Subscription Model` is a specific recurring-revenue structure.
- `Fractionalization`: customers buy access to or a fraction of an asset rather than full ownership.[^ci-bm-p42]

Enrich existing pricing nodes within the same comparison. `Price Discrimination` charges different prices across customer or product groups; `Dynamic Pricing` changes prices as conditions change; `Bundling` sells several products together; `Razor-and-Blades Model` combines a base product with repeat purchases; `Penetration Pricing` sacrifices initial unit economics to accelerate adoption; and `Freemium Model` offers a free tier intended to support conversion or another revenue source. The slide also lists auction pricing, leasing, licensing, and franchising as related structures.[^ci-bm-p42]

### Value chain, profitability, and unit economics

Enrich `Value Chain` as the systems and processes performed within a company to create value for customers, and distinguish it from `Supply Chain`, which traces the broader flow across organizations and counterparties.[^ci-bm-p43]

Enrich `Operating Profit Margin`, `Unit Economics`, and `Break-Even Point` as the three profitability lenses in the source. Operating margin assesses profitability at company level, unit economics compares revenue and cost on a per-unit basis, and break-even identifies the activity level at which total revenue covers total cost. Link these notes to the relevant Equity and FSA applications without adding formulas not present on this slide.[^ci-bm-p43]

### Business-model variations and networks

Enrich `Private Label`, `Licensing`, `Franchise`, and `Value-Added Reseller` as business-model variations. Private-label or contract manufacturers produce for another brand; licensing grants defined rights; franchise models replicate a business format through franchisees; and value-added resellers supplement another producer's offering with added services or configuration.[^ci-bm-p44]

Enrich `Platform Business Model` and `Network Effect`. A network effect means the network becomes more valuable to users as participation grows. Create `One-Sided Network Effect` and `Multi-Sided Network Effect`: the former strengthens among one user group, while the latter connects two or more participant groups whose participation raises value for the other sides.[^ci-bm-p44]

Enrich `Crowdsourcing`: users contribute directly to the product or service rather than acting only as purchasers or audience members.[^ci-bm-p44]
