Section divider: Market Organization and Structure / 金融市场.

## PDF page 4

Financial-system functions include saving/borrowing, raising equity, risk transfer, exchange, information discovery, and agency-problem resolution. Regulation supports investor protection, fair markets, financial stability, and systemic-risk control; a well-functioning market is complete and operationally and informationally efficient.

## PDF page 5

Intermediaries: brokers locate counterparties for commissions; block brokers serve large traders; investment banks support securities issuance; exchanges supply venues. Alternative trading systems include ECNs in the United States and MTFs in Europe.

## PDF page 6

Dealers trade as principals, provide liquidity, and earn the bid–ask spread; broker-dealers combine agency and principal roles. Securitizers pool assets, arbitrageurs trade price discrepancies, deposit institutions channel savings, insurers pool risk, and settlement/custody institutions complete and safeguard trades.

## PDF page 7

Markets may be classified by security type, maturity, issue status, settlement timing, or mechanism. Primary markets issue new securities through IPOs or seasoned offerings; underwritten offerings transfer placement risk to the bank, whereas best-efforts offerings do not.

## PDF page 8

Primary-market alternatives: private placements, shelf registrations, dividend reinvestment plans, and rights offerings.

## PDF page 9

Secondary markets may be call markets, with periodic batched clearing, or continuous markets, where trades occur whenever counterparties meet. Call markets concentrate liquidity; continuous markets favour immediacy.

## PDF page 10

Quote-driven markets use dealer quotes; brokered markets use search, especially for unique/large orders; order-driven markets match public orders by rules. Priority may reflect price, display, and time; pricing can be uniform or discriminatory.

## PDF page 11

Asset classes surveyed: securities, currencies, contracts, commodities, and real assets. Securities include fixed-income, equity, pooled, and securitized instruments; contracts include forwards, futures, options, swaps, and insurance.

## PDF page 12

Long positions gain from price increases; short positions gain from declines. Long options have limited downside and optionality; short options receive premium but assume exercise risk.

## PDF page 13

Short selling borrows and sells securities, later repurchasing them for return to the lender. The short seller posts collateral, may receive a rebate net of fees, and owes payments in lieu of distributions.

## PDF page 14

Margin buying uses a margin loan; buyer’s equity is the investor-funded portion. Leverage is position value divided by equity, and margin rate is its reciprocal. Visually verified margin-call price: $P_c=P_0(1-\text{initial margin})/(1-\text{maintenance margin})$.

## PDF page 15

The bid is the highest standing buy price and ask the lowest sell price; the difference is the spread. Order instructions specify execution, validity, and clearing/settlement.

## PDF page 16

Market orders prioritise immediacy; limit orders constrain price. A marketable limit order can execute immediately. All-or-none requires full execution; hidden and iceberg orders conceal all or part of size.

## PDF page 17

Validity instructions include day, good-till-cancelled, immediate-or-cancel, fill-or-kill, on-open, and on-close. Stop orders activate at a trigger price.

## PDF page 20

Section divider: Security Market Indexes / 指数.

## PDF page 21

An index contains constituent securities and uses a divisor. Visually verified: $PR_1=(V_1-V_0)/V_0=\sum_iw_iPR_i$; $TR_1=(V_1-V_0+D_1)/V_0=\sum_iw_iTR_i$. Indexes gauge sentiment, benchmark managers, proxy for asset classes/systematic risk, and support index products.

## PDF page 22

Index categories include broad/style equity, sector, fixed-income, real-estate, hedge-fund, and commodity indexes; construction and data biases differ.

## PDF page 23

Price weighting averages constituent prices and can be replicated with equal share counts. It is simple but high-priced stocks dominate; splits require divisor adjustment.

## PDF page 24

Market-cap weighting uses each company’s share of total market value; large firms dominate and can create momentum tilt. Float adjustment excludes closely held shares; free-float adjustment also excludes shares unavailable to foreign investors.

## PDF page 25

Equal weighting gives equal weights and arithmetic-mean returns but creates small-cap bias and rebalancing needs. Fundamental weighting uses price-independent measures such as book value, cash flow, revenue, earnings, dividends, or employees, producing value/contrarian tilts.

## PDF page 26

Rebalancing restores target weights; reconstitution changes constituents. Both create turnover and costs.

Section divider: Market Efficiency / 市场有效性.

## PDF page 29

An informationally efficient market incorporates information rapidly and rationally. Efficiency is supported by informed participants, low information/trading costs, and limited arbitrage impediments.

## PDF page 30

Weak form incorporates past trading data; semi-strong form incorporates public information; strong form incorporates public and private information.

## PDF page 31

Anomalies include calendar effects, momentum, and overreaction. Persistence, post-cost significance, risk compensation, and data-mining must be assessed.

## PDF page 32

Cross-sectional anomalies include size and value effects; explanations can include risk, frictions, and behavioural mispricing.

## PDF page 33

Other anomalies include closed-end-fund discounts and IPO patterns. Limits to arbitrage can permit persistence.

## PDF page 34

Behavioural explanations include overconfidence, representativeness, and cascades/herding.

## PDF page 35

Mental accounting separates fungible wealth into buckets; conservatism slows belief updating; narrow framing evaluates decisions outside total-portfolio context.

## PDF page 37

Section divider: Overview of Equity Securities / 权益证券.

## PDF page 38

Common shares represent residual ownership and generally vote; dividends are discretionary. Callable common lets the issuer repurchase under specified terms; putable common gives holders a sale-back right.

## PDF page 39

Preferred shares rank ahead of common for dividends/liquidation but usually have limited voting rights. Variants include cumulative, participating, and convertible preferred.

## PDF page 40

Private equity includes venture capital, leveraged buyouts, and private investments in public equity. Depositary receipts represent interests in foreign shares.

## PDF page 41

Sponsored depositary receipts involve the issuer; unsponsored receipts do not. ADRs trade in the US and GDRs in multiple markets. ADR Level I trades OTC; Levels II/III list, and Level III can raise capital.

## PDF page 42

Preferred is generally less risky than common; putable, cumulative, and participating features alter risk/return. ROE is accounting return, cost of equity is required market return; book equity differs from market equity.

## PDF page 44

Section divider: Introduction to Industry and Company Analysis / 行业与公司分析.

## PDF page 45

An equity research report includes business/industry description, forecasts, valuation, recommendation, and risks.

## PDF page 46

A business model explains value creation/capture. Revenue may be forecast bottom-up from operational units or top-down from market size, share, and growth.

## PDF page 47

Pricing power is the ability to raise price without proportionate demand loss; drivers include differentiation, switching costs, substitutes, customer concentration, and rivalry.

## PDF page 48

Cost analysis separates operating, investing, and financing costs and fixed versus variable behaviour.

## PDF page 49

Economies of scale reduce average cost as output grows; economies of scope reduce cost by producing related products together.

## PDF page 50

Working-capital analysis links receivables, inventory, and payables to the cash conversion cycle; sources and uses explain capital flows.

## PDF page 51

ROIC relative to required return helps assess value creation. ROIC/ROA are unlevered-style measures; leverage converts operating into equity returns. Visually verified: $ROE=\text{net margin}\times\text{asset turnover}\times\text{equity multiplier}$.

## PDF page 52

Operating risk is operating-income sensitivity to units sold and rises with fixed operating costs. Visually verified: $DOL=\%\Delta EBIT/\%\Delta Q=Q(P-V)/[Q(P-V)-F]=(EBIT+F)/EBIT$.

## PDF page 53

Financial leverage comes from fixed financing costs. Visually verified: $DFL=\%\Delta NI/\%\Delta EBIT=EBIT/(EBIT-I)$; $DTL=DFL\times DOL=\%\Delta NI/\%\Delta Q=(EBIT+F)/(EBIT-I)$.

## PDF page 54

Industry analysis defines peers and evaluates structure, growth, profitability, competition, and cyclicality.

## PDF page 55

Classification systems may be government/statistical or commercial; GICS, ICB, and TRBC group firms by business activity.

## PDF page 56

Industries can also be grouped by geography, cycle sensitivity, return co-movement, or ESG characteristics.

## PDF page 57

Industry surveys estimate demand, supply, capacity, pricing, growth, and profitability; scope, sampling, definitions, and private-company omissions matter.

## PDF page 58

Profitability is best assessed with a time series/distribution of ROIC. Market shares should be ranges. Visually verified concentration: $HHI=\sum_i s_i^2$; rising concentration may indicate weaker competition and higher profitability.

## PDF page 59

Porter’s Five Forces: rivalry, entry, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. PESTLE: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental.

## PDF page 60

Competitive strategies include broad/focused cost leadership and differentiation.

## PDF page 61

Forecasting translates business/industry analysis into revenue, expense, investment, financing, and statement expectations; objects, horizon, and scenarios should be explicit.

## PDF page 62

Historical forecasting extrapolates past levels, trends, or relationships but can fail under structural change, cyclicality, or mean reversion.

## PDF page 63

Base-rate forecasting uses a reference class; convergence forecasting moves unusual performance toward sustainable levels.

## PDF page 64

Management guidance and discretionary analyst forecasts can add timely information but require bias and consistency checks.

## PDF page 65

Revenue forecasts separate price, volume, mix, market growth, and market share; risks include optimistic share, inconsistent macro assumptions, and capacity limits.

## PDF page 66

Operating expenses may be fixed/variable or driver-linked. Working capital uses operational ratios; capex should separate maintenance from growth.

## PDF page 67

Leverage forecasts connect debt, interest, covenants, and cash capacity. Scenario/sensitivity analysis tests statement, valuation, and solvency effects.

## PDF page 70

Section divider: Equity Valuation—Concepts and Basic Tools / 权益估值.

## PDF page 71

Valuation families: present value (DDM, FCFE, FCFF, residual income), market multiples (P/E, P/B, P/S, P/CF, EV multiples), and asset-based valuation.

## PDF page 72

Distributions include dividends and repurchases; stock dividends/splits change shares and per-share price without directly creating value. Dates: declaration, ex-dividend, record, payment.

## PDF page 73

Visually verified: $V_0=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_i/(1+r)^i$; Gordon $V_0=D_1/(r-g)$ with constant $g<r$, and $g=(1-\text{payout})ROE$. Two-stage DDM adds high-growth dividends and terminal value; preferred stock $V_0=D/r$.

## PDF page 74

FCFE valuation: $V_0=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}FCFE_t/(1+r)^t$. FCFE is dividend-paying capacity and can value non-dividend payers. Price multiples use earnings/book/sales/cash flow; trailing uses current/historical and leading uses forward denominators.

## PDF page 75

Visually verified justified P/E: $P_0/E_1=(1-b)/(r-g)$ and $P_0/E_0=(1-b)(1+g)/(r-g)$. P/E falls with required return and rises with growth; payout effects can be ambiguous. Multiples are intuitive but sensitive to accounting, cyclicality, and comparables.

## PDF page 76

$EV/EBITDA$ uses $EV=$ common equity + preferred equity + debt − cash/equivalents and aids comparison across capital structures. Asset-based equity value is market assets minus liabilities; it suits tangible/readily valued or liquidating firms.
