Section divider: Portfolio Risk and Return—Modern Portfolio Theory / 现代投资组合理论.

## PDF page 4

Single-asset holding-period return combines income and price change. Expected return is probability-weighted return; variance/standard deviation measure dispersion. Investors may be risk-averse, risk-neutral, or risk-seeking.

## PDF page 5

Visually verified utility: $U=E(R)-0.5A\sigma^2$, with $A>0$ risk-averse, $A=0$ neutral, and $A<0$ seeking. Indifference curves contain equal-utility risk/return pairs; for risk-averse investors they slope upward and are convex, with curvature increasing in risk aversion.

## PDF page 6

Visually verified two-asset formulas: $E(R_p)=w_1R_1+w_2R_2$ and $\sigma_p^2=w_1^2\sigma_1^2+w_2^2\sigma_2^2+2w_1w_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rho_{12}$. Diversification lowers risk when $\rho<1$; with $\rho=-1$, $\sigma_p=|w_1\sigma_1-w_2\sigma_2|$.

## PDF page 7

The minimum-variance frontier traces minimum risk at each expected return; its upper part is the efficient frontier. The investor’s optimal risky portfolio is the tangency between the efficient frontier and the highest attainable indifference curve.

## PDF page 8

The capital allocation line combines a risk-free asset and one risky asset. Visually verified: $E(R_p)=r_f+[(R_A-r_f)/\sigma_A]\sigma_p$; the slope is the risky asset’s Sharpe ratio, and the highest-slope feasible CAL is preferred.

## PDF page 9

The optimal CAL is tangent to the risky-asset frontier at optimal risky portfolio Y; investor P combines Y with the risk-free asset according to risk preference. Under homogeneous expectations, the common optimal risky portfolio is the market portfolio. Visually verified CML: $E(R_p)=r_f+[(R_M-r_f)/\sigma_M]\sigma_p$.

## PDF page 10

Systematic risk affects the market and cannot be diversified away; unsystematic risk can. Visually verified: $\beta_i=\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_M)/\sigma_M^2=\rho_{iM}\sigma_i/\sigma_M$ and $\beta_p=\sum_iw_i\beta_i$. Only systematic risk is priced in the model.

## PDF page 11

Return-generating models include multifactor models using macroeconomic, fundamental, or statistical factors, and single-index models. Visually verified: $E(R_i)-r_f=\beta_i[E(R_M)-r_f]$; market model $R_i=\alpha_i+\beta_iR_M+e_i$. The security characteristic line has beta as its slope.

## PDF page 12

CAPM assumes risk-averse rational utility maximisers, one common holding period, homogeneous expectations, price-taking, infinitely divisible investments, and frictionless/taxless markets. Visually verified: $E(R_i)=r_f+\beta_i[E(R_M)-r_f]$.

## PDF page 13

The SML plots expected return against beta; intercept is $r_f$ and slope is the market risk premium. Properly priced assets lie on it, overpriced below, underpriced above. The CML applies to efficient portfolios and total risk; the SML applies to all properly priced assets/portfolios and systematic risk.

## PDF page 14

Visually verified performance measures: Jensen’s $\alpha_p=R_p-[r_f+\beta_p(E(R_M)-r_f)]$; Treynor $(R_p-r_f)/\beta_p$; $M^2=(R_p-r_f)\sigma_M/\sigma_p+r_f$; $M^2$ alpha $=M^2-R_M$; Sharpe $(R_p-r_f)/\sigma_p$.

## PDF page 17

Section divider: Portfolio Management—Overview / 投资组合管理概述.

## PDF page 18

A portfolio approach evaluates holdings jointly rather than in isolation. The process includes planning, execution, and feedback: understand the client and IPS, allocate/select/implement, then monitor and rebalance.

## PDF page 19

Investor types differ in objectives and constraints. Defined-contribution participants bear investment risk; defined-benefit sponsors promise benefits. Endowments/foundations, banks, and insurers have different liquidity, horizon, liability, regulatory, and tax profiles.

## PDF page 20

Open-end mutual funds issue/redeem at NAV; closed-end funds trade on exchanges and may deviate from NAV. ETFs trade intraday and use creation/redemption mechanisms that help keep price near NAV.

## PDF page 21

The asset-management industry includes active and passive traditional strategies and alternatives. Trends include indexing, fee pressure, technology, product innovation, and increasing attention to client outcomes.

## PDF page 22

An investment policy statement records governance, client circumstances, return objectives, risk objectives, and constraints. The constraint mnemonic is TTLLU: time horizon, taxes, liquidity, legal/regulatory, and unique circumstances.

## PDF page 23

Strategic asset allocation sets long-term target weights; tactical allocation makes shorter-term deviations. Portfolio construction implements the allocation with securities/vehicles, while risk budgeting distributes acceptable risk across components.

## PDF page 24

ESG integration considers environmental, social, and governance information. Approaches can include negative/positive screening, best-in-class selection, thematic or impact investing, active ownership, and full ESG integration.

## PDF page 25

Recap of the portfolio approach/process, investor types, pooled vehicles, asset-management industry, IPS, allocation, risk budgeting, and ESG.

## PDF page 26

Section divider: Behavioral Biases / 行为金融.

## PDF page 27

Behavioural biases are grouped into cognitive errors and emotional biases. Cognitive errors arise from faulty reasoning or information processing and may be moderated with education; emotional biases stem from feelings and are often harder to correct.

## PDF page 28

Cognitive errors include belief perseverance (conservatism, confirmation, representativeness, illusion of control, hindsight) and processing errors (anchoring/adjustment, mental accounting, framing, availability).

## PDF page 29

Emotional biases include loss aversion, overconfidence, self-control, status quo, endowment, and regret aversion. They can influence risk taking, trading, diversification, and reluctance to rebalance.

## PDF page 30

Behavioural effects on markets include momentum, bubbles/crashes, value/growth mispricing, excessive trading, and limits to arbitrage. Biases can affect both individual decisions and aggregate prices.

## PDF page 31

Recap of cognitive/emotional classifications, individual biases, and market effects.

## PDF page 32

Section divider: Risk Management / 风险管理.

## PDF page 33

A risk-management framework establishes governance, infrastructure, policies/processes, risk appetite/tolerance, identification, measurement, modification, and monitoring. Enterprise risk management considers interactions among risks rather than isolated silos.

## PDF page 34

Risk identification covers financial risks—market, credit, liquidity—and non-financial risks such as operational, model, settlement, legal, regulatory, political, and reputational risk.

## PDF page 35

Risk measurement can use probability, volatility, sensitivity, scenario/stress tests, value at risk, and conditional VaR/expected shortfall. Credit-risk measurement considers exposure, default likelihood, and loss severity.

## PDF page 36

Risk treatment choices include avoidance, acceptance, prevention/reduction, transfer, and insurance/hedging. Decisions should compare costs, benefits, residual risk, and alignment with tolerance.
