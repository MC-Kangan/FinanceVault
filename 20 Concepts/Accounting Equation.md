# Accounting Equation

> [!summary]
> The accounting equation states that assets are financed by liabilities and owners' equity, keeping the balance sheet in balance.[^fsa-l1-p4]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula and mechanics

$$
\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Owners' Equity}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p4]

For changes between two balance-sheet dates:[^fsa-l1-p4]

$$
\Delta A = \Delta L + \Delta E
$$
[^fsa-l1-p4]

### Interpretation

Every transaction that changes one side of the equation must be offset by another change so that the equality remains intact.[^fsa-l1-p4]

### Common pitfalls

The equation is an identity, not a valuation statement: it explains how recorded assets are financed but does not by itself show whether book values equal market values.[^fsa-l1-p4]

## Connections

- The identity organizes the [[Balance Sheet]].
- Owners' equity becomes the denominator of [[Return on Equity]].
