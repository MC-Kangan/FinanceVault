# Return on Equity

> [!summary]
> Return on equity (ROE) measures net income relative to average total equity and can be decomposed to reveal profitability, efficiency, and leverage drivers.[^fsa-l1-p46][^fsa-l1-p47]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula

$$
\text{ROE}=\frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Average Total Equity}}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p46]

### Interpretation

ROE reflects the return generated on the average book equity base. A change in ROE may come from operating profitability, asset use, financing leverage, interest burden, or tax burden rather than from one cause.[^fsa-l1-p47]

### Common pitfalls

A high ROE is not automatically evidence of strong operations because a smaller equity base or greater financial leverage can raise the ratio.[^fsa-l1-p47]

## Connections

- Use [[DuPont Analysis]] to separate ROE into drivers.
- [[Net Profit Margin]] is one of the three-part decomposition factors.
- Average equity is anchored in the [[Accounting Equation]] and [[Balance Sheet]].
