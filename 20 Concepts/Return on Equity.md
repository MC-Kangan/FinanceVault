# Return on Equity

> [!summary]
> Return on equity (ROE) measures net income relative to average total equity and can be decomposed to reveal profitability, efficiency, and leverage drivers.[^fsa-l1-p46][^fsa-l1-p47]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula

$$
\text{ROE}=\frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Average Total Equity}}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p46]

### Interpretation

ROE reflects the return generated on the average book equity base. A change in ROE may come from operating profitability, asset use, financing leverage, interest burden, or tax burden rather than from one cause.[^fsa-l1-p47]

### Common pitfalls

A high ROE is not automatically evidence of strong operations because a smaller equity base or greater financial leverage can raise the ratio.[^fsa-l1-p47]

### Cross-topic applications

<!-- merged:eq-pm-applications -->

**Equity Investments application:** ROIC relative to required return helps assess value creation. ROIC/ROA are unlevered-style measures; leverage converts operating into equity returns. Visually verified: $ROE=\text{net margin}\times\text{asset turnover}\times\text{equity multiplier}$.[^eq-l1-p51]

### Equity slide enrichment

Compare long-run invested-capital returns with investors' required returns. ROIC and ROA are unlevered measures; financial leverage produces levered equity returns. The slide preserves the three-step decomposition:[^eq-enrich-p51]

$$
ROE=Net\ profit\ margin\times Total\ asset\ turnover\times Equity\ multiplier
$$[^eq-enrich-p51]
