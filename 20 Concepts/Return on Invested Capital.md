## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula and mechanics

**Equity Investments — Industry and Company Analysis:** ROIC relative to required return helps assess value creation. ROIC/ROA are unlevered-style measures; leverage converts operating into equity returns. Visually verified: $ROE=\text{net margin}\times\text{asset turnover}\times\text{equity multiplier}$.[^eq-l1-p51]

### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** ROIC compares after-tax operating profit with average invested capital and indicates value creation when it exceeds the cost of capital. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Capital Investments]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p27]

<!-- enrichment:equity:start -->

### Equity slide enrichment

Compare long-run invested-capital returns with investors' required returns. ROIC and ROA are unlevered measures; financial leverage produces levered equity returns. The slide preserves the three-step decomposition:[^eq-enrich-p51]

$$
ROE=Net\ profit\ margin\times Total\ asset\ turnover\times Equity\ multiplier
$$[^eq-enrich-p51]

[^eq-enrich-p51]

### Slide-level enrichment

$$
ROIC=\frac{After\text{-}tax\ operating\ profit}{Average\ invested\ capital}
=After\text{-}tax\ operating\ margin\times Capital\ turnover
$$
[^ci-l1-p27]

The source defines invested capital as average long-term liabilities plus equity and excludes working capital. ROIC above the cost of capital indicates value creation. ROIC is accessible to outside analysts but is accounting-based, backward-looking, volatile, and highly aggregated.[^ci-l1-p27]
