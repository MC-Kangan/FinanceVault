## Level 1 - Foundation

### Definition and mechanics

- A fixed-rate bond's return comes from promised coupons and principal, reinvestment income on coupons, and any capital gain or loss when sold before maturity.[^fi-risk-p60]
- Interest-rate risk has two opposing components: coupon reinvestment risk matters more over long horizons, while market-price risk matters more over short horizons.[^fi-risk-p60]

### Exam focus

Identify the definition, direction of the principal relationships, and the financial-statement or valuation effect tested by this concept.[^fi-risk-p60]

### Slide-deck enrichment

<!-- merged:fi-enrichment -->

Fixed-rate returns come from promised cash flows, reinvestment income, and capital gain or loss. Reinvestment risk matters more over long horizons; market price risk matters more over short horizons. Macaulay duration is the horizon at which the two effects offset for a one-time parallel curve shift.[^fi-risk-p60][^fi-risk-p61]
