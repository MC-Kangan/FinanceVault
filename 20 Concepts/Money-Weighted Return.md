# Money-Weighted Return

> [!summary]
> Money-weighted return is the internal rate of return that weights performance by the size and timing of investor cash flows.[^quant-l1-p6]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula and mechanics

Money-weighted return is the internal rate of return that weights performance by the size and timing of investor cash flows.[^quant-l1-p6]

### Interpretation

Use this concept within [[Quant - Interest Rates and Investment Returns]] and distinguish its role from neighbouring measures or methods before applying a formula.[^quant-l1-p6]

### Common pitfalls

Match the definition, inputs, direction, and units to the question; do not substitute a similarly named statistic or return measure.[^quant-l1-p6]

### Alternative Investments context

Money-weighted return is the internal rate of return used to evaluate cash flows with their size and timing.[^alt-l1-p8]

### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** IRR is the discount rate that makes NPV zero; non-conventional cash flows can create multiple or nonexistent solutions. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Capital Investments]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p26]

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

IRR is the discount rate satisfying $0=CF_0+\sum_{t=1}^{n}CF_t/(1+IRR)^t$. It communicates profitability, but non-conventional cash flows may produce multiple IRRs or no IRR and the method assumes reinvestment at the IRR. For conventional independent projects, NPV and IRR agree: accept when NPV is positive and IRR exceeds the cost of capital.[^ci-l1-p26]
