### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** The cash conversion cycle combines inventory days and receivable days less payable days; supplier discounts imply an annualized cost of forgone trade credit. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Working Capital and Liquidity]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p18]

<!-- enrichment:equity:start -->

### Equity slide enrichment

A shorter cash-conversion cycle reduces external funding needs, while negative working capital makes suppliers a financing source. Capital sources include operating cash flow, debt, equity, and asset disposals; uses include liquidity, positive working capital, capital expenditure, acquisitions, debt repayment, dividends, and repurchases.[^eq-enrich-p50]

<!-- enrichment:equity:end -->

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

The operating cycle is $DOH+DSO$; the cash conversion cycle is $DOH+DSO-DPO$ and measures the time from paying suppliers for inventory to collecting cash from sales. A shorter or negative cycle indicates more efficient operations in the source.[^ci-l1-p18]

For trade terms `a/m, net n`, the annualized cost of forgoing the discount is:[^ci-l1-p18]

$$
EAR=\left(1+\frac{a\%}{100\%-a\%}\right)^{\frac{365}{n-m}}-1
$$
[^ci-l1-p18]

Do not treat the discount percentage itself as the borrowing cost without adjusting the base and annualizing the period.[^ci-l1-p18]
