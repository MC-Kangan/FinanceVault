# Opportunity Cost

> [!summary]
> Opportunity cost is the value forgone by choosing one course of action over another.[^quant-l1-p4]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula and mechanics

Opportunity cost is the value forgone by choosing one course of action over another.[^quant-l1-p4]

### Interpretation

Use this concept within [[Quant - Interest Rates and Investment Returns]] and distinguish its role from neighbouring measures or methods before applying a formula.[^quant-l1-p4]

### Common pitfalls

Match the definition, inputs, direction, and units to the question; do not substitute a similarly named statistic or return measure.[^quant-l1-p4]

### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** Capital budgeting uses after-tax incremental cash flows, includes opportunity costs and externalities, excludes sunk costs, and accepts positive-NPV independent projects. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Capital Investments]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p25]

### Economics applications

<!-- merged:economics-applications -->

**Economics application:** Demand slopes downward under the law of demand; economic profit subtracts both explicit and implicit opportunity costs, while normal profit makes economic profit zero. Use [[Economics - Firms and Market Structures]] for chapter context.[^eco-l1-p4]

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

Capital budgeting uses timed, after-tax incremental cash flows; includes positive or negative externalities and opportunity costs; and excludes sunk costs. Net present value is:[^ci-l1-p25]

$$
NPV=CF_0+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}
$$
[^ci-l1-p25]

For an independent project, accept if $NPV>0$ and reject if $NPV<0$. NPV directly estimates the change in firm value but does not communicate project size.[^ci-l1-p25]
