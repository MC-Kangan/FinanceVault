## Level 1 - Foundation

### Definition and mechanics

- For nonfinancial firms, distinguish uncommitted and committed credit lines, revolving credit, secured asset-based loans, factoring, and commercial paper; commercial paper is unsecured and commonly exposes the issuer to rollover risk.[^fi-markets-p25]
- For banks, cover retail and commercial deposits, negotiable and non-negotiable certificates of deposit, interbank funds, central-bank facilities, commercial paper, and asset-backed commercial paper.[^fi-markets-p27][^fi-markets-p28]

### Exam focus

Identify the definition, direction of the principal relationships, and the financial-statement or valuation effect tested by this concept.[^fi-markets-p25]

### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** Short-term funding should provide adequate, diversified, competitively priced credit while reflecting issuer quality, collateral, and legal constraints. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Working Capital and Liquidity]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p21]

### Slide-deck enrichment

<!-- merged:fi-enrichment -->

Companies use credit lines, revolving facilities, secured asset-based loans, factoring, and unsecured commercial paper. Investment-grade debt is more bond-like with lower credit-spread contribution to YTM; high-yield debt is more equity-like, commonly secured, and more restrictive.[^fi-markets-p25][^fi-markets-p26]

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

Short-term funding should maintain sufficient and diversified credit sources, preserve capacity for changing cash needs, secure competitive terms, and include implicit as well as explicit costs. Strategy depends on company size and creditworthiness, legal and regulatory constraints, and available collateral.[^ci-l1-p21]
