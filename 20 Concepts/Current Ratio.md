# Current Ratio

> [!summary]
> The current ratio is a liquidity ratio that compares current assets with current liabilities to assess short-term obligation coverage.[^fsa-l1-p45]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula

$$
\text{Current Ratio}=\frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p45]

### Interpretation

A higher ratio indicates more reported current assets per unit of current liabilities, but interpretation still depends on the quality and convertibility of those assets.[^fsa-l1-p45]

### Common pitfalls

The ratio is a balance-sheet snapshot. It does not by itself reveal the timing of cash receipts and payments or the composition of current assets.[^fsa-l1-p45]

### Corporate Issuers and Derivatives applications

<!-- merged:ci-der-applications -->

**Corporate Issuers application:** Liquidity drags slow inflows, liquidity pulls accelerate outflows, and current, quick, and cash ratios measure progressively narrower liquid resources. Use [[Corporate Issuers - Working Capital and Liquidity]] for the surrounding chapter context.[^ci-l1-p17]

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

Liquidity drags slow cash inflows through uncollected receivables, obsolete inventory, or borrowing constraints. Liquidity pulls accelerate outflows through early payment, reduced trade-credit limits or credit lines, and chronic shortages. The liquidity ratios narrow from current to quick to cash, and higher values generally indicate more liquidity.[^ci-l1-p17]

$$
Current\ ratio=\frac{Current\ assets}{Current\ liabilities},\quad
Quick\ ratio=\frac{Cash+Marketable\ securities+Receivables}{Current\ liabilities},\quad
Cash\ ratio=\frac{Cash+Marketable\ securities}{Current\ liabilities}
$$
[^ci-l1-p17]
