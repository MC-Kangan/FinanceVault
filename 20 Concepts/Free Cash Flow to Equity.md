# Free Cash Flow to Equity

> [!summary]
> Free cash flow to equity (FCFE) is cash available to common equity after debt financing costs and net borrowing are reflected.[^fsa-l1-p40]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Formula from FCFF

$$
\text{FCFE}=\text{FCFF}-\text{Interest}(1-t)+\text{Net Borrowing}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p40]

### Formula from cash flow from operations

$$
\text{FCFE}=\text{CFO}-\text{FCInv}+\text{Net Borrowing}
$$
[^fsa-l1-p40]

### Interpretation

Subtracting after-tax interest removes the debt holders' financing claim, while net borrowing adds cash raised from creditors net of debt repayment.[^fsa-l1-p40]

### Common pitfalls

Net borrowing can be negative when debt repayment exceeds new borrowing, reducing cash available to equity holders.[^fsa-l1-p40]

### Cross-topic applications

<!-- merged:eq-pm-applications -->

**Equity Investments application:** Valuation families: present value (DDM, FCFE, FCFF, residual income), market multiples (P/E, P/B, P/S, P/CF, EV multiples), and asset-based valuation.[^eq-l1-p71]

**Equity Investments application:** FCFE valuation: $V_0=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}FCFE_t/(1+r)^t$. FCFE is dividend-paying capacity and can value non-dividend payers. Price multiples use earnings/book/sales/cash flow; trailing uses current/historical and leading uses forward denominators.[^eq-l1-p74]

<!-- enrichment:equity:start -->

### Equity slide enrichment

The FCFE model values equity from dividend-paying capacity and can value non-dividend-paying shares:[^eq-enrich-p71][^eq-enrich-p74]

$$
V_0=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{FCFE_t}{(1+r)^t}
$$[^eq-enrich-p71][^eq-enrich-p74]

The model-selection slide separately identifies FCFF as a present-value approach to firm cash flows.[^eq-enrich-p71][^eq-enrich-p74]
