# Debt Financing

> [!summary]
> Debt Financing is a Level 1 concept in [[Corporate Issuers]]; its separate note preserves a reusable definition and a clear destination for later-level extensions.[^ci-l1-p7]

## Level 1 - Foundation

### Definition and mechanics

Debt and equity differ in issuer cost and leverage and in investor claim priority, maturity, voting rights, downside, and return potential.[^ci-l1-p7]

### Interpretation

Identify the role of Debt Financing within its contract, decision rule, governance mechanism, financing choice, or operating framework, and distinguish it from adjacent concepts.[^ci-l1-p7]

### Common pitfalls

<!-- enrichment:corporate-issuers:start -->

### Slide-level enrichment

From the issuer perspective, debt has a lower cost but creates more financial leverage and suits predictable performance; equity costs more but creates less financial leverage and suits less predictable performance. From the investor perspective, debt has finite tenor, prior fixed claims, capped return, lower risk, and no voting rights; equity has indefinite tenor, residual claims, potentially unlimited return, higher risk, and voting rights. The simplified source comparison caps maximum loss at the initial investment for both.[^ci-l1-p7]
